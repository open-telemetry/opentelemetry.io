The OpenTelemetry blog is one of our most valuable communication avenues with
our community. The web is changing, though, and we need to change with it. Like
many websites, we've seen organic search collapse: 50% fewer visitors arrive on
opentelemetry.io via Google today than they did in January. This doesn't mean
that traffic is down, though. Over the last 30 days, we've had over 15 million
page views, nearly a million unique visitors, and 1.4 TB of bandwidth usage.

Unsurprisingly, many of our users are electing to use AI tools to access
information, news, and documentation about the project. This shift in
consumption requires us to step back and reevaluate where we're spending our
time and energy. This year we've seen an increase in blog posts, and a
commensurate increase in maintainer demands for content and editorial review of
those posts. This not only takes time, but it's also less valuable than ever
before. Agents and models are using our documentation directly; many efforts
around blogging could be better spent on documentation!

This is not to say that we're removing or winding down the blog, but we'll be
instituting two policy changes. First, you will need to be a member in good
standing of the OpenTelemetry organization to submit a blog post. Second, we
will strongly prefer blog posts relating to organizational or project
announcements — for example, releases or other milestones, SIG formation or
progress, and so on.

We believe that there are many avenues to publishing blog content about
OpenTelemetry, and we would like to continue to support authors and content
creators in the community. Please
[join the discussion](https://github.com/open-telemetry/opentelemetry.io/discussions/12694)
if you have ideas or suggestions about how we can surface and promote your
content.
